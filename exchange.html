The story

At 16, I left Bangkok for a school year in rural Minnesota — population ~1,000 in Madison Lake. It was my first time living abroad, my first American Thanksgiving, my first time playing in a school wind ensemble — and the first time I had to do calculus, biology, and band class all in English at the same time.

What I studied

Science

Biology

Hands-on labs in a U.S. high school setting — dissections, microscope work, and lab reports in English. Built my comfort with technical vocabulary I didn't have before.

Math

AP Calculus

First calculus course — and in a second language. The math symbols were universal but the word problems forced me to slow down and translate before solving.

Athletics

Advanced PE

First exposure to American sports culture — team-based PE was very different from PE back home. Pushed me out of my comfort zone physically and socially.

Music

Wind Ensemble

Joined the school's wind ensemble. Music turned out to be the easiest language to share — sitting in a section with classmates was where I made my first real friends.

What I brought home

  • Comfort with ambiguity — learning to function when you don't fully understand the rules yet, in language or in social settings.
  • Asking better questions — when you can't assume context, you learn to ask before deciding. That habit shows up directly in how I approach data work and user research today.
  • Cross-cultural communication — translating not just words but expectations, humor, and what people actually mean vs. what they say.
  • Resilience under loneliness — a small Minnesota town in winter is a long way from Bangkok. The year built a quiet confidence I still rely on.
