The goal

Standard Value-at-Risk (VaR) models — the tool banks and hedge funds use to size positions and reserve capital — miss roughly 1 in 4 stress-period losses. They assume markets crash gently and react symmetrically to news. This project rebuilds VaR around two facts real markets actually obey: news sentiment matters, and bad news hits harder than good. The result is a model that catches the crashes Gaussian VaR systematically misses.

Case study

United Airlines, June 2025 — Strait of Hormuz closure

A geopolitical news shock (Iran conflict, closure of the Strait of Hormuz) drove UAL down 9.5% in a single day — the steepest decline in 10 months — closing at $106.30. Markets reacted instantly to sentiment, while traditional Gaussian VaR models flagged nothing.

What we built

A news-shock framework that maps daily sentiment signals to equity volatility, using a downside-asymmetric Student-t distribution to capture the leverage effect — where negative news triggers exponentially stronger reactions than positive news. Built and validated across Nike, Exxon Mobil, and Google as a 5-person team capstone for the Zanista industry partner.

Limitations & what's next

  • Data: RavenPack only — no social media, earnings calls, or macro signals yet
  • Granularity: Daily data misses intra-day reactions; next step is intraday signals
  • Coverage: Validated on 3 stocks; extending to FX, crypto, and bonds
  • Model flex: Future work explores BERT for text and regime-switching tail models
